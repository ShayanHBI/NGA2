# Finite-rate relaxation and phase-change literature: working bibliography

Prepared **2026-11-02**. Broad curated bibliography, **not a provably exhaustive Google Scholar export**. Direct comprehensive Google Scholar crawling was unavailable. Sources: publishers, authors’ publication lists, academic indices, and Pelanti (2022) references. The last two groups are broader context, **not necessarily studies of finite-rate relaxation**.

**A:** direct finite-rate and mathematical relaxation. **B:** empirically or physically calibrated phase transition timescales, HRM and DEM. **C:** related equilibrium-limit and solver papers. **D:** interfacial microphysics or foundations.

The short comments describe likely relevance; they are not claims that every listed paper contains a rate formula.

## A. Direct finite-rate multiphase relaxation and mathematically grounded models

1. **Pelanti (2022)**, [Arbitrary-rate relaxation techniques for the numerical modeling of compressible two-phase flows with heat and mass transfer](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmultiphaseflow.2022.104097). *Central uploaded article; empirical flashing coefficient Eq. 84.*
2. **De Lorenzo, Lafon & Pelanti (2019)**, [A hyperbolic phase-transition model with non-instantaneous EoS-independent relaxation procedures](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2018.12.002). *Earlier finite-rate pressure/heat/mass; interfacial area.*
3. **De Lorenzo et al. (2021)**, [A hyperbolic phase-transition model coupled to tabulated EoS for two-phase flows in fast depressurizations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nucengdes.2020.110954). *Tabulated-EOS non-instantaneous relaxation.*
4. **Sirianni & Re (2026)**, [Efficient solution of mechanical and thermo-chemical finite-rate relaxation for compressible liquid-vapor flows under arbitrary equations of state](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2025.114467). *Finite pressure/velocity/thermal/chemical; nozzle droplet-size-dependent nonlinear closures.*
5. **Pelanti (2026)**, [Numerical relaxation techniques for mass transfer in three-phase liquid-vapor-gas flows](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compfluid.2025.106893). *Finite-rate liquid-vapor-gas extension.*
6. **Pelanti (2024)**, [Numerical Modeling of Liquid-Vapor-Gas Flows with Arbitrary-Rate Mass Transfer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-55264-9_16). *Short earlier conference version of 2026 work.*
7. **Chiocchetti & Müller (2020)**, [A solver for stiff finite-rate relaxation in Baer-Nunziato two-phase flow models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09381). *Exponential matrix-based relaxation solver.*
8. **Schmidmayer et al. (2023)**, [Modelling interactions between waves and diffused interfaces](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.5142). *Finite mechanical relaxation; interface thickness/acoustic timescale.*
9. **Hurisse & Quibel (2021)**, [Simulations of liquid-vapor water flows with non-condensable gases on the basis of a two-fluid model](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apm.2021.06.020). *Very relevant noncondensable-gas relaxations and timescale sensitivity.*
10. **Re & Abgrall (2022)**, [A pressure-based method for weakly compressible two-phase flows under a Baer–Nunziato type model with generic equations of state and pressure and velocity disequilibrium](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.5087). *Finite pressure and velocity coefficients, area/viscosity dependence.*
11. **Damianos, Papoutsakis, Karathanassis & Gavaises (2026)**, [Effect of gas nuclei on the primary stage of shock–droplet interaction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmultiphaseflow.2025.105478). *Shock cylindrical droplet, finite pressure relaxation and air nuclei.*
12. **Biasiori-Poulanges & Schmidmayer (2023)**, [A phenomenological analysis of droplet shock-induced cavitation using a multiphase modeling approach](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0127105). *Cylindrical shock-droplet cavitation with finite relaxation.*
13. **Schmidmayer & Biasiori-Poulanges (2023)**, [Geometry effects on the droplet shock-induced cavitation](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0151404). *Relaxation-enabled shock cavitation; droplet geometry effects.*
14. **Bidi et al. (2022)**, [Numerical study of real gas effects during bubble collapse using a disequilibrium multiphase model](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultsonch.2022.106175). *Real-gas bubble collapse with disequilibrium multiphase model.*
15. **Bussac & Mathis (2023)**, [Simulation of a homogeneous relaxation model for a three-phase mixture with miscible phases](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2023.05.012). *Liquid, vapor, inert gas; entropy-gradient and BGK relaxation.*
16. **Bresch, Burtea & Lagoutière (2022)**, [Mathematical justification of a compressible bifluid system with different pressure laws: a continuous approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06497). *Physical relaxation terms derived via homogenization, linked to viscosity.*
17. **Bresch & Hillairet (2019)**, [A Compressible Multifluid System with New Physical Relaxation Terms](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.2387). *Multifluid effective physical relaxation.*
18. **Perrier & Gutiérrez (2021)**, [Derivation and closure of Baer and Nunziato type multiphase models by averaging a simple stochastic model](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1306609). *Stochastic average of mesoscale topology.*
19. **James & Mathis (2016)**, [A relaxation model for liquid-vapor phase change with metastability](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06333). *Metastable phase-change mathematical relaxation.*
20. **Demou, Scapin, Pelanti & Brandt (2022)**, [A pressure-based diffuse interface method for low-Mach multiphase flows with mass transfer](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2021.110730). *Finite-rate sources supported in model; many tests equilibrium.*
21. **Zamani Salimi, Mukherjee, Pelanti & Brandt (2025)**, [A low Mach number diffuse-interface model for multicomponent two-phase flows with phase change](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2024.113683). *Gas mixtures and interfacial phase change.*
22. **Sirianni, Re & Abgrall (2025)**, [Mixture-conservative temperature-based Baer–Nunziato solver for efficient full-disequilibrium simulations of real fluids](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compfluid.2025.106761). *Related full-disequilibrium solver.*
23. **Linga & Flåtten (2019)**, [A hierarchy of non-equilibrium two-phase flow models](https://doi.org/10.1051/proc/201966006). *Flow hierarchy and relaxation physics.*
24. **Flåtten & Lund (2011)**, [Relaxation Two-Phase Flow Models and the Subcharacteristic Condition](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218202511005775). *Thermodynamic admissibility and wave speeds.*
25. **Lund (2012)**, [A Hierarchy of Relaxation Models for Two-Phase Flow](https://doi.org/10.1137/12086368X). *Thermodynamic hierarchy.*
26. **Martínez Ferrer, Flåtten & Munkejord (2012)**, [On the effect of temperature and velocity relaxation in two-phase flow models](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2011039). *Finite and instantaneous velocity/temperature equilibrium comparison.*
27. **Aursand et al. (2014)**, [An exponential time-differencing method for monotonic relaxation systems](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=An+exponential+time-differencing+method+for+monotonic+relaxation+systems). *Numerical exponential relaxation methodology.*
28. **Han, Hantke & Müller (2017)**, [Efficient and robust relaxation procedures for multi-component mixtures including phase transition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2017.02.066). *Multicomponent phase-transition algorithms.*
29. **Collis et al. (2026, preprint)**, [An LES model with finite-rate phase change and subgrid spray based on a thermodynamically consistent four-equation multiphase model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23846). *Finite-rate phase-change linked to interfacial area and subgrid spray.*
30. **Heuzé & Favrie (online 2026; scheduled 2027 issue)**, [Variational formulation of the constitutive relaxation at finite rate of the mechanical state in multiphase flows with the diffuse-interface method](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.119417). *Dissipation-based mechanical relaxation closure, not numerical correlation.*
31. **Alekseev (2023)**, [Numerical algorithms for solving two-phase flows based on relaxation Baer-Nunziato model](https://doi.org/10.26089/NumMet.v24r214). *Numerical BN; Russian-language paper.*

## B. Homogeneous Relaxation Model (HRM), Delayed Equilibrium Model (DEM), and experiment-derived rates

2. **Downar-Zapolski, Bilicki, Bolle & Franco (1996)**, [The non-equilibrium relaxation model for one-dimensional flashing liquid flow](https://doi.org/10.1016/0301-9322(95)00078-X). *Empirical time correlation for flashing water.*
